## 1. Purpose, scope & non-goals

Carol's gym programs sections by the clock: "5 rounds, one every 5:00", "AMRAP 15", "20 minute cap, for time". This spec is the screens for Spec 09.5. A section's **timer** is chosen on the section sheet that already holds its name and note (**Timer: None / Set Timer / AMRAP / Time Cap**, plus the plan numbers), in the routine editor and in a workout (live, and on a finished workout's edit screen). After each round she opens **Log result** on the timed section's heading and types the gym clock's reading as digits (`341` is 3:41), or the rounds + reps she reached. Those entries are **queued on the phone like sets** and sent when there is signal. The score then shows on the heading, the workout summary and History. **Routine from text** reads the gym app's timing lines into a timer when, and only when, the section's score is a time (or, for an AMRAP, rounds + reps). Timers are optional everywhere; an untimed section looks and behaves exactly as today.

There is **no live clock** in this spec (design §4): the lifter reads the gym's clock and types. Nothing here makes a later clock harder (times are plain seconds, the `round` op is keyed by round number).

> **First-principles note: why digits typed from the right.** On an iPhone the number pad has no colon key. A "mm:ss" text box would make the lifter switch keyboards between the minutes and the seconds. Calculator-style entry avoids it: the field holds only digits, the last two are always the seconds, and the colon is drawn for you. Typing 3, 4, 1 shows `0:03`, `0:34`, `3:41`. That is three taps for a round time, with the pad that is already open, in one hand.

> **First-principles note: why the queued ops name a round, not "the next round".** The outbox may send an op twice (the reply is lost, the app retries). "Set round 3 to 3:41" can be applied twice with the same outcome; "add a round" cannot. Spec 09.5 built the routes that way (`PUT …/round-times/3`). This spec only has to queue *final values keyed by round*, in order, and never merge two ops in a way that changes what the server sees in between (§6.6).

> **First-principles note: what "a superset" means here.** A superset is a set of exercises logged in turn. In SIN it is only a label on workout/routine exercises (`superset_group`) that the screens draw as a bracket and that the Hevy export turns into a `superset_id`. Carol wants the exercises of a timed section grouped, because one round is "one set of each". The group is a *default the screens apply*, not a rule the server enforces (09.5 D7), so this spec says exactly when the screens apply it (§6.4). A timed section can hold more than one superset ("Directly into" pairs), so the default only fills in where there is none.

## 2. Acceptance criteria

Behavioral criteria each get ≥1 test whose `describe` is tagged **`"10.7 ACn — …"`**, enforced by a scan test in the style of `acTags.10-6.test.ts` (AC65). "Live" means `ActiveSession`; "edit" means `EditWorkoutScreen`. "The projection" means the cached workout with the outbox's queued ops overlaid (`project`, Spec 06.2). Seconds are whole numbers. Levels are in §10.

### Phase A — shared timer pieces and the routine side

**The clock field**

1. **Digits fill from the right.** The clock field (`ClockField`) is a text input with `inputMode="numeric"` that holds only digits, at most five. The last two digits are the seconds and the rest the minutes, and the field shows `m:ss` as typed: `3`, `4`, `1` show `0:03`, `0:34`, `3:41`; `1`, `2`, `3`, `4`, `5` show `123:45`; a sixth digit is ignored; Backspace drops the last digit; non-digit characters are ignored; a pasted `3:41` or `341` is accepted (the colon is dropped); an empty field means no value. The pure module `sectionTimer/clockDigits.ts` has `digitsToSeconds`, `digitsToText` and `secondsToDigits`, and `secondsToDigits(digitsToSeconds(d))` returns `d` without leading zeros for every value 1–10 800.
2. **Seconds go up to 59.** Digits whose last two form 60–99 (`375`) give no value and show the field error `Seconds go up to 59.`; the typed digits stay visible so the lifter fixes one digit. A value above the field's maximum is the form-level message of AC5, never a silent clamp.
3. **Field accessibility.** The field has a visible label, the hint `Type the digits: 341 is 3:41.` linked by `aria-describedby`, any error linked and announced the way the app's `Field` does, and is at least `--tap-target-min` tall. The mobile keyboard hint is `inputMode="numeric"` with `autoComplete="off"`; the field does not autofocus.

**The timer group**

4. **Timer fields.** `TimerFields` renders a native `<select>` labeled **Timer** with the options `None`, `Set Timer`, `AMRAP`, `Time Cap`, and below it by format: **Set Timer** — **Rounds** (a plain numeric input, 1–100) and **Round length** (a clock field, 0:01–60:00); **AMRAP** — **Time limit** (a clock field, 0:01–180:00); **Time Cap** — **Cap** (a clock field, 0:01–180:00, may be empty) with the hint `Leave empty for For Time, with no cap.`; **None** — nothing. Choosing another format empties the fields of the others; choosing None drops the plan. A section with no timer opens on None with nothing below.
5. **Advisory validation.** Pressing the sheet's commit button validates the group with texts from `timerCopy.ts`: `Enter the round length.`, `Enter the number of rounds.`, `Enter the time limit.`, `Rounds go from 1 to 100.`, `Keep the round length to 60:00 or less.`, `Keep it to 180:00 or less.`, plus AC2's seconds text; all errors show at once under their fields and nothing is committed. The bounds come from `@sin/core`'s constants (`TIMER_ROUND_SECONDS_MAX`, `TIMER_ROUNDS_MAX`, `TIMER_CAP_SECONDS_MAX`), never from a literal in `apps/web`.
6. **Fields ⇄ timer.** The pure `sectionTimer/timerFields.ts` converts the fields to a `SectionTimer | null` or a list of field errors, and a `SectionTimer | null` to fields; the two are inverse for every format and boundary (a Time Cap with no cap round-trips as `capSeconds: null`; the request form omits the key when serialized by the caller, per 09.5 AC2).
7. **One line component.** `TimerLine` renders a section's plan as `describeTimer(timer)` and, when given a score, ` · ` + `scoreText(score)`; every screen that shows a plan or a score uses it. A source scan finds no clock or score formatting in `apps/web` outside `@sin/core`'s `timer.ts` and the clock field's own digit display (09.5 AC3's "no second copy").

**The routine editor**

8. **Draft and body.** `DraftSection` gains `timer: SectionTimer | null`. The draft's `load` takes it from `Routine.sections[].timer`; `toRoutineInput` emits `sections.<j>.timer` only when it is non-null, so a draft with no timers sends a body byte-identical to today's (the recorded request bodies of Specs 10.2 / 10.3 replay equal) and a `PUT` that drops a timer simply omits it (09.5 AC6). A new section starts with `timer: null`.
9. **The routine section sheet.** The editor's section sheet (`routines/SectionSheet.tsx`) gains the Timer group under Note. **Done** parses name, note and timer together (AC5) and commits all or nothing; **Close** discards. A timed section's editor header shows its `TimerLine` under the name (above the note) and stays reachable by the existing name button; an untimed header is unchanged.
10. **Server field errors.** A `422` on `sections.<j>.timer.<field>` (the server's static messages are never shown) maps onto the sheet's matching field with the AC5 texts through `routineErrors`, opens nothing new, and a `422` on `sections.<j>.timer` alone shows `Check the timer and try again.` under the group.
11. **Preview.** The routine preview shows each timed section's `TimerLine` under its heading and nothing for an untimed one; the existing preview snapshots pass unmodified.

**Grouping**

12. **Grouping rules in core.** `@sin/core` `supersets.ts` gains two pure functions and a `SessionOp` `{ type: "groupRange", from, to }` handled by `planSessionChange`. `groupRangeRule(slots, from, to)`: the slots `from..to` (inclusive, display order) are **replaced** by one run — the first `min(n, 8)` get one fresh label, the rest of the range get no group — labels canonical; a range of one slot gets no group (a group of one draws no bracket); slots outside the range are untouched; `planSessionChange` returns the minimal patch list (untouched runs keep their numbers) and `capped: true` when `n > 8`. `timedGroupRange(segment, trigger)` decides **whether and what** to group, where `segment` is one section's slots and `trigger` is `"fill"` (the section became timed or changed format) or `{ join: id }` (an exercise was added or moved in): `fill` returns the whole segment's range **iff no slot is in a run of ≥ 2** (any existing grouping is kept as it is); `join` looks at the *other* slots and returns the whole segment's range iff they are **exactly one superset** — every other slot in one run of ≥ 2 — or there is **exactly one other slot** (a lone exercise is a group of one); otherwise `null` (the new exercise stays loose); `null` also for an empty or one-slot segment. Unit-tested over: 0, 1, 2, 8, 9, 10 slots; already grouped (no patches); two supersets (fill → `null`, join → `null`); one superset of 3 plus a loose one (join → `null`); one superset of 8 (join → groups the first 8, `capped`, the new one loose); a lone other slot; an ungrouped 3 (fill → one run, join → `null`).
13. **Editor coupling.** In the routine editor, a section **becomes timed** (None → a format) or **changes format** → `timedGroupRange(items, "fill")`: the section's items become one superset only if none of them is grouped; **adding an item** to a timed section and **moving an item into one** (`MoveToSectionSheet`) → `timedGroupRange(items, { join })`: the new item joins only when the others are exactly one superset or one lone exercise, otherwise it stays loose. With more than 8 items the first 8 are grouped and the section shows the muted line `Only the first 8 exercises are grouped.`. A plain rename / note edit, a change of the numbers within the same format, and choosing None change no group; a section with two supersets keeps both; the lifter can still link, unlink and regroup freely and the screen never relinks except on these triggers. `settle` is unchanged (this is an action step, not an invariant).
14. **Round trip and rollout.** The fake API's `PUT /v1/routines/{id}` echoes `timer`; saving a routine with each format and reopening it yields the same plan (deep equal); the web parses `RoutineSection.timer` as required (no `.default`, `.catch`; the strict-parse tests and the 10.1 AC26 scan stay green); fixtures gain `timer: null`.

### Phase B — the timer on a workout's sections, the display, the coupling

**The sheets**

15. **Timer group on the workout sheets.** The New section and Edit section sheets of Spec 10.6 gain the same `TimerFields` below Note. **Add section** sends `POST /v1/workouts/{id}/sections` with `{ name, notes?, timer? }` (`timer` only when set). **Save** on Edit sends only the groups that changed: `name`, `notes`, and `timer` (a `SectionTimer`, or `null` to remove); nothing changed closes and sends nothing. Every other 10.6 rule (no autofocus, the double-tap guard, announcements, focus return) is unchanged; the status line says `Timer saved` when the timer changed (with or without a name or note change) and `Section saved` otherwise.
16. **A timer change that would clear recorded data.** The screen asks for confirmation whenever the projection shows any round time or a result for the section **and** the change would not keep it: the new choice is None, a different format, or the same format whose numbers no longer contain what is recorded (`timerFitsRecorded` returns an issue). Save then opens a `ConfirmDialog` — title `Change {name}'s timer?`, body `This clears the times and result you logged for this section.`, **Cancel** (focused) and a danger **Change and clear** — and sends nothing until **Change and clear**, which sends the `PATCH` with `discardResult: true`. **Cancel** returns focus to Save and changes nothing. With nothing recorded there is no dialog and no `discardResult`; a same-format change that still contains what is recorded is sent plainly (the data is kept, and `discardResult` is never sent).
17. **Same format, numbers that do not fit use the same flow.** Fewer rounds than recorded, a round length or cap below a recorded time, a cap removed from a capped result: the screen decides with `timerFitsRecorded` (its issues are not shown; `code`s are only for the field-level wording of AC56) and runs AC16's confirm and `discardResult` — there is no "clear those times first" step. A server `409 section-result-exists` for such a change (a stale screen) is AC18.
18. **A stale screen.** `409 section-result-exists` (another device logged times after this screen loaded) refetches the workout, keeps the sheet open and shows `Times were logged for this section. Check them and try again.`; nothing is cleared.

**Headings and display**

19. **The heading.** On the live and edit screens a timed section's heading shows, under its name and Edit button, its `TimerLine` (plan, and ` · ` score when there is one); under that, when any round times are recorded, the **times line** (AC20's format); and a secondary **Log result** button (accessible name `Log result for {name}`, ≥ `--tap-target-min`). Only a real section's **first** segment carries the button, exactly like Edit (`editable`); the synthetic **Other exercises** heading, an untimed section and a read-only caller never show it. An untimed heading is unchanged.
20. **The summary.** `FinishedWorkoutScreen` draws a timed section's `TimerLine` and times line under its heading and note, read-only, from the same `sectionScore` / `scoreText`: the times line is `Rounds 3:41 · 3:38 · 3:30` for a Set Timer and `Round ends 4:10 · 8:35 · 12:50` for an AMRAP / Time Cap, in round order, a skipped round between two recorded ones shown as `–`; the line is absent when nothing is recorded. No button appears. An untimed section's markup is byte-identical to today's (the existing snapshots pass unmodified).
21. **History rows.** A `HistoryRow` whose workout has timed sections **with a score** (`timedSections[].score !== null`) shows, below the meta line, one line per such section in position order — `{section name} · {describeTimer} · {scoreText}` — at most two, then a muted `+{n} more` when there are more; unscored timed sections never appear; a row with none is byte-identical to today's (the `HistoryRow.parity` test passes unmodified). The row stays one link; the lines wrap rather than truncate.

**Grouping in a workout**

22. **When a workout groups.** After the `PATCH` that makes a section timed or changes its format succeeds and the detail is re-read, `timedGroupRange(segment, "fill")` runs on that section's segment; after **adding an exercise** that the server placed in a timed section (10.6's C4 line) and after a **Section-select move into a timed section**, once the detail shows the exercise there, `timedGroupRange(segment, { join: id })` runs. A non-null range is sent through `useRegroup.run` with `groupRange` (positions untouched, patches only for exercises whose group changes). Nothing runs for a name or note edit, a same-format number change, removing the timer, or a section with fewer than two exercises; a section that already holds two supersets, or loose exercises mixed with a superset, keeps exactly the groups it has. The regroup is online-only (no outbox) and obeys the screen's `structureLock`.
23. **When grouping fails.** If the timer saved (or the exercise was added or moved) and the regroup fails (offline, `4xx`, `5xx`), the screen keeps the change, shows the banner `Timer saved, but the exercises couldn't be grouped — try again` (for an add or move: `Exercise added, but it couldn't be grouped — try again`, `Exercise moved, but it couldn't be grouped — try again`) with a **Try again** button that recomputes `timedGroupRange` from the current detail and re-runs it (idempotent: an already-grouped segment sends no patch), and never rolls the change back. More than 8 exercises groups the first 8 and the heading shows the muted line `Only the first 8 exercises are grouped.`

**Writes and screens**

24. **Online-only, same discipline as 10.6.** The timer writes are the existing section mutations (`networkMode: "always"`, `retry: false`, no outbox, drafts or rest-store access) taking the screen's `structureLock` and counting in `structureBusy`. Live success invalidates only `["workouts","active"]`. Live events: `section_timer_set { format }` (`format` is the enum value, `none` for removal) once per timer change; no seconds, name or note.
25. **Failures.** `resolveSectionFailure` (Spec 10.6 AC13) gains the timer cases by status and problem slug only: `422` on `timer.<field>` → the AC5 field text, `409 section-result-exists` → AC18 (the server no longer answers `422` on `timer` for a change that does not fit, 09.5 AC13), `409 section-timer-mismatch` does not occur on these routes and falls into the generic row; every other case is 10.6's table unchanged. The offline text, rate-limit text and request id are the screen's.
26. **The edit screen.** The edit screen renders the same Timer group, confirm, heading, **Log result** button (its sheet is AC42), grouping and failures through the same components, with `useEditWorkoutClient()`, `EDIT_MUTATION_KEY`, the returned `WorkoutSection` written into `["workouts","detail",id]` (`sections`, by `position`), and `editSucceeded` on every success. `workout_edit { edit: "section_timer_changed" }` is emitted (the server's enum, 09.5 D21); edit mode emits no live event. A regroup there re-reads the detail (`useRegroup` with the edit options, 18.0 AC11). The edit screen is still a sibling of `ActiveSession`, never a mode of it.

### Phase C — Log result and the offline outbox

**The sheet**

27. **Log result.** The heading's **Log result** opens a full-height `Sheet` titled `Log result`, showing the section name and its `TimerLine`, a body by format (AC28–AC30), and **Cancel** / **Save**. The sheet's text is local state seeded from the projection once per opening (keyed by an open counter) and never stored; it does not autofocus; the first empty round row carries the visible tag `Next` and is scrolled into view. A section that disappears while the sheet is open (another device removed it) closes the sheet with `That section no longer exists`; one whose timer was removed closes it with `This section has no timer now.`
28. **Set Timer body.** One clock field per planned round (`Round 1` … `Round N`, N ≤ 100), each empty or a time ≤ the round length; the line `Total` shows `scoreText(sectionScore(draft))` and updates as the lifter types (`Total 14:03`; nothing when no round has a time). A time over the round length shows the field error from `roundTimeIssues`. Fewer rounds than planned is legal.
29. **AMRAP body.** **Rounds** and **Reps** `NumberStepper`s (0–999, 0–9 999), both required to save a result (blank until touched; a saved result with 0 rounds is legal), and an optional **Round ends** list of clock fields (`Round 1`, `Round 2` …) with an **Add round time** button that appends the next number (the highest shown + 1, up to 100) and a hint `Type the gym clock when each round ended.`. Readings must be later than the previous and earlier than the next and within the time limit (errors from `roundTimeIssues`); empty rows are ignored.
30. **Time Cap body.** A native radio group **Finished** / **Hit the cap**, the second only when the timer has a cap (a "For Time" section shows no radio). **Finished:** a clock field **Finish time** (≤ the cap when there is one). **Hit the cap:** optional **Rounds** and **Reps** steppers, at least one required (`Give rounds or reps.`). **Round ends** as in AMRAP. Switching the radio empties the other mode's fields in the draft.

**Saving**

31. **Save.** Save validates every field with the core helpers (`roundTimeIssues` per changed round against the projected neighbors, `resultIssues`), shows all errors inline with focus on the first, and sends nothing while any exists. With no error it queues the changes (AC32, AC34) and **closes at once** (local-first, like logging a set), the status line saying `Result saved`. With nothing changed it closes and queues nothing. A change from the saved values is by value, per round and for the result as a whole.
32. **Write order.** For several changed rounds the sheet queues them in an order in which each write is valid against the previous state: first the **clears**, then the rounds whose value **went down**, ascending by round, then the rounds whose value **went up or are new**, descending by round, then the result. The pure `orderRoundWrites(existing, finalValues)` (Set Timer rounds are independent and keep ascending order) is property-tested: for random valid `existing` and `final` states of an AMRAP / Time Cap, applying its output one write at a time through `roundTimeIssues` never reports an issue.
33. **Clearing.** Emptying a recorded round's field and saving queues that round's clear (`DELETE`); a **Clear result** button (shown when a result exists) empties the result fields in the draft and Save queues the result's clear; clearing the result leaves the round times and clearing a round leaves the result. **Cancel** discards the draft.

**The outbox ops**

34. **Op shapes and persistence.** `OutboxOp` gains `round` — `{ kind: "round", sectionId, round, body: { seconds: number | null } }` (`null` = clear) — and `result` — `{ kind: "result", sectionId, body: SetSectionResult | null }` (`null` = clear). Both carry the common fields (`id`, `workoutId`, `userId`, `status`, `attempted`, `attempts`, `nextAttemptAt`, `enqueuedAt`, `failure?`) and no `workoutExerciseId`; `OpBase` is split so the set and note ops are byte-identical on disk. `OutboxFileSchema` (still `v: 1`, the same `sin:workout:outbox` key) parses them; a persisted file with them restores after a reload; ops of another user are discarded as before (06.2 AC8); nothing else about storage changes.
35. **Combine.** A new `round` / `result` op **replaces the body of the existing op** only when that op has the same key (`sectionId` + `round`, or `sectionId` for the result), is the **last queued op of that section**, is `queued` and not `attempted` — or is `failed` with a `422` (reset to queued, like a note, Spec 10.3 AC12); in every other case it is appended. Ops of different sections or of sets and notes never merge with it. Replaying the queue in order therefore reproduces what the lifter did, state by state (D7).
36. **Classification.** `classify` gains the new kinds: network error, `5xx`, `408`, `429` → retry; `404` → done (the section or workout is gone; the cache is re-read); `409 section-timer-mismatch`, any `422`, any other status → fail (permanent). A failed op stays in the queue as `failed` with its failure; later ops of other keys are unaffected; the `409 workout-finished` cascade of 06.2 applies to the new kinds too.
37. **Draining and cleanup.** The new ops are sent by the existing drainer: FIFO with every other op, one in flight, the same backoff and triggers (06.2 AC4–AC6). Success of a `PUT` writes the returned `WorkoutSection` into `["workouts","active"]` (`sections`, by position) and removes the op; success of a `DELETE` (`204`) removes that round or the result from the cached section; the `404` path invalidates `["workouts","active"]`. Ops of a workout are removed on finish, delete and the gone path as 06.2 AC17 / 18.0 say; `settle` keeps unsent ops.
38. **Projection.** `project()` overlays pending `round` / `result` ops, in queue order, onto the cached section's `roundTimes` (kept sorted by round) and `result` (the full object, `capped` false by default, `null` for a clear) — so the heading, the sheet, the score and every core helper read the same state — and marks the section id in the `sync` map (`pending`, or `failed` with the op id and status). Pure and idempotent: projecting an already projected workout is the same workout (06.2 AC1).
39. **Sync UI and the Finish gate.** A section with unsent entries shows `Not saved yet` after its score (text, not color alone); with a failed one, `Couldn't be saved`. `SyncStatus` counts them as `timer entries` (`1 timer entry`, `3 timer entries`) beside sets and notes ("2 sets and 3 timer entries"), with Retry now and Discard (the Discard's accessible name names them); Finish is disabled while any remain with the hint `{n} timer entries not saved yet`, in the order sets, notes, timer entries, the way the note hint was added (06.2 AC16, 10.3 AC29). Failed entries are discarded by the existing **Discard**, which removes the op and so reverts the projection.
40. **No timer change under unsent entries.** While the outbox holds any `round` / `result` op for a section, its Edit sheet's Timer group is disabled with the line `Wait for the logged times to save before changing the timer.`; name and note remain editable. (A timer change is online-only and immediate; a queued entry validated against the old timer could fail permanently, §6.6.)
41. **Offline and replay.** With the browser offline, **Save** in Log result still queues and closes; the heading shows the projected score with `Not saved yet`; reconnecting drains in order and the tag goes away; delivering each queued op to the (fake) server **twice** leaves the same stored state and the same cache as once (09.5 AC18's guarantee carried through); a reload with ops pending restores them and drains after sign-in.

**The edit screen and events**

42. **Edit screen path.** On the finished-workout edit screen the **Log result** sheet is the same component; **Save** sends the AC32 sequence on the plain REST client (`useEditWorkoutClient()`, `EDIT_MUTATION_KEY`, `networkMode: "always"`, `retry: false`), one request after another, stopping at the first failure; each success writes the returned section into `["workouts","detail",id]`; a `DELETE` removes the entry from the cached section; every success runs `editSucceeded` (so History, Progress and records refresh); `workout_edit { edit: "section_result_changed" }` once per Save. Offline shows `EDIT_COPY.offline`; the failure table is AC25's, the outbox, drafts and rest store are never touched (18.0 AC21's test and AC46's scan are extended to the new files), and a failure mid-sequence re-reads the detail and shows `Couldn't save all of the result — check it and try again`.
43. **Live events.** Live **Save** emits `section_result_logged { format }` once per Save (not per round); a sync failure emits the existing `set_sync_failed { status }` once per failed entry; no event carries seconds, reps, a name or an id.

## 6. Behavior & logic

### 6.1 Clock entry

`clockDigits.ts` (pure): `digitsToSeconds(d)` = `parseInt(m) * 60 + ss` when the last two digits are < 60, else `null`; `digitsToText(d)` = `m:ss` with the minutes un-padded and the seconds padded (`""` for an empty string); `secondsToDigits(s)` is the inverse. The field's value is the digit string; every edit is a pure function of `(digits, input event)`. The maximum is five digits (minutes up to 999 are typed, 180:00 is the largest accepted value of any field in AC5; 60:00 for a round length). A field shows `m:ss` as typed (`75:00`), while every read-out elsewhere is `formatClock` (`1:15:00`, 09.5); this inconsistency exists only for plans over an hour (D1).

### 6.2 `TimerFields` and the group's value

`timerFields.ts`: `fieldsFromTimer(timer)` → `{ format, rounds, roundDigits, capDigits }`; `timerFromFields(fields)` → `{ ok: true, timer } | { ok: false, errors }`. The Time Cap cap is the only field that may be empty. The select is a controlled value; choosing a format resets the other formats' fields (AC4).

### 6.3 Saving a timer change (workout)

A pure `decideTimerSave(stored, next, recorded)` returns one of: `unchanged`; `send` (`{ timer }`); `confirm` (`{ timer, discardResult: true }` after the dialog) — chosen when something is recorded and the format differs, the new choice is None, or `timerFitsRecorded` returns an issue for the same format. `recorded` is `roundTimes.length > 0 || result !== null` read from the projection. The sheet sends `timer: null` to remove. After a successful send the screen re-reads (live: refetch; edit: the response is the section) and then runs the coupling of §6.4.

### 6.4 Superset coupling (D5, D21)

A timed section can hold more than one superset (Carol, O2), so grouping is a **fill-in**, never a rewrite. Rules, identical in the routine editor and in a workout, computed by core's `timedGroupRange`:

| Event | Effect |
|---|---|
| Section **becomes timed** or **changes format** | if **none** of its exercises is grouped: one superset over all of them (cap 8). If any grouping exists: nothing |
| Exercise **added to**, or **moved into**, a timed section | joins only when the other exercises are **exactly one superset** (all in one run) or there is **one** other exercise (then the two are grouped); otherwise it stays loose |
| Name / note edit; same-format number change; timer removed | nothing |
| Lifter links / unlinks by hand | allowed; never undone by the screen |
| Section with < 2 exercises | nothing to group (a group of one is no group) |
| Section with > 8 exercises | first 8 grouped; the line `Only the first 8 exercises are grouped.` |

Examples: a new AMRAP section of 3 loose exercises → one superset of 3; Second Shift (two supersets of two) made Time Cap → unchanged; a fifth exercise added to it → stays loose; a section that is one superset of 3, a fourth exercise added → joins (4); a section of 3 loose exercises the lifter had unlinked on purpose, a fourth added → stays loose.

In the routine editor the step is part of the reducer action (atomic with the edit). In a workout it is a second request sequence after the structure write succeeded and the detail was re-read (so it always works from the server's truth); `useRegroup.run(slots, { type: "groupRange", from, to }, { positionOffset })` feeds one segment's slice, exactly as 10.3's section-bounded moves do, and sends the minimal `PATCH` list one at a time. A split section (two segments, 09.2 §6.5) is decided per segment.

### 6.5 The Log result draft and the writes

`resultDraft.ts` (pure) builds the draft from the projected section (`fromSection`), derives `scoreText` for the live total, validates (`validateDraft` → `{ path, message }[]` through the core helpers, mapped to copy by `timerIssueCopy`), and `diff(projected, draft)` → the changes: per round `set(round, seconds)` / `clear(round)`, and `setResult(body)` / `clearResult()`. `orderRoundWrites` (AC32) orders the round changes. Saving:

- **Live:** `enqueueRound` / `enqueueResult` in that order (each resolves at once), then close.
- **Edit:** the same sequence over REST, awaited one by one.

The draft is built for the section's format only: Set Timer → rounds; AMRAP → rounds, reps, readings; Time Cap → mode, finish / (rounds, reps), readings. A result is never queued for a Set Timer (its score is derived, 09.5 AC16) and a `round` op is never queued for an untimed section; the sheet cannot offer either.

### 6.6 The outbox additions

- **Why only the last op of a section may be replaced (D7).** Spec 09.5 checks each write against the server's *current* neighbors (strictly increasing readings). A queue `[round 2 := 8:35]`, `[round 1 := 4:10]` is valid in that order only if each op was validated against the state the ones before it produced. If a later edit could be folded into an *earlier* op, the earlier op might carry a value that is only valid because of a later op, and the server would refuse it in between. Replacing only the last queued op of the same section and key keeps every queue prefix a state the lifter actually passed through. Appending extra ops costs a few bytes and a few requests (≤ 100 rounds per section).
- **Validation uses the projection.** Every client check (`roundTimeIssues`, `resultIssues`) runs against the projected section (server copy + queued ops), so a write that is valid in the lifter's view is valid when replayed in order.
- **`classify`**: as AC36; the table is the 09.5 §3 table.
- **Ordering with a timer change.** A timer change is an immediate online write; a queued entry validated against the old timer could be refused (`409 section-timer-mismatch` or `422`) and lost. So the Timer group is disabled while the section has unsent entries (AC40). A timer changed on *another device* meanwhile makes the queued entries fail permanently; the heading shows `Couldn't be saved`, **Discard** reverts the display, and the lifter re-enters them.
- **`onSectionSynced`** is the provider's callback (the `onNoteSynced` pattern): it receives the op and the returned section (or `null` for `204` / `404`) and updates the active-workout cache (AC37).
- **Telemetry**: `track` stays at the scalar events of AC43; `set_sync_failed` keeps counting failed entries.

### 6.8 The reader (Phase D)

**Cleaning.** In the cleaning loop, where a badge line marks the line above it `tagged`, the badge text is stored on that line (`CleanLine.badge`), and `badges` is returned in step with `tagged`. Overlap handling copies it with the tag. A header with no visible badge (cut off at a screenshot's edge) has `null`.

**Score kind.** As AC45. The `Score:` line is read from the section's note lines (it already lands there).

**Timer rules**, applied once per section after its note lines are known (the reader's loop already collects them in `MutableSection.notes`), on the collapsed line:

## 7. Security & privacy

- **No new authority.** Every call is Spec 09.5's, authenticated and scoped server-side; the client sends a section id it received and a round number the sheet drew.
- **Text as text.** Section names and notes are rendered only as React text children or attributes; the new strings are static constants (AC56, AC58). Times and counts are numbers rendered by `formatClock` / `scoreText`.
- **No leakage.** Seconds, reps and rounds are health-adjacent training data; they never reach `track`, `reportError`, `reportUnexpected` or the console (AC58). The outbox already stores workout data on the device under the existing key and clears it at logout (Spec 06.0 `USER_DATA_KEY_PREFIXES`).
- **No new storage.** As AC58. The reader runs on the device with no external service (Spec 10.4).
- **Abuse bounds.** At most 100 rounds × 8 sections of queued ops per workout (09.5 §7); the form limits mirror the API's.

### Resolved: this spec's calls (mine)

- ✅ **D1 (mine) — Calculator-style digit entry for every clock.** Digits only, the last two are seconds, the colon is drawn. **Rationale:** the iPhone number pad has no colon; this is three taps for 3:41 with no keyboard switch, and the same field serves plan numbers and results. **Cost:** a plan over an hour displays as `75:00` in the field and `1:15:00` elsewhere; a user who thinks "341 means 341 seconds" sees `3:41` immediately. **Rejected:** a `mm:ss` text input (colon problem); separate minute and second boxes (twice the taps and focus moves); a stepper-only time (too slow for `3:41`).
- ✅ **D2 (mine) — Four phases, in the order A routines → B workout structure and display → C Log result and the outbox → D the reader.** **Rationale:** each is one session; A and B give a lifter a working timed section before any offline queue exists; C is the riskiest (outbox) and is isolated; D needs only the draft's `timer` and is independent of B and C. **Rejected:** one PR; the outbox ops as their own phase (they have no screen without the sheet).
- ✅ **D3 (mine) — A neutral `features/sectionTimer/`.** `routines/` and `workouts/` may not import each other (10.1 AC28), yet both need the Timer group and the line. **Rationale:** the same shape as `catalog/newExerciseName.ts`.
- ✅ **D4 (mine) — Two outbox op kinds, `round` and `result`, not one.** Different keys (section + round vs section), different bodies, and the combine rule differs by key. `OpBase` is split because these ops have no `workoutExerciseId`. **Rejected:** a single `timing` kind with a union body (the key logic would branch inside every function).
- ✅ **D5 (mine; reworked on Carol's O2 answer, 2026-11-01) — Superset coupling is a fill-in default applied at triggers, never an invariant and never a rewrite.** A timed section can hold several supersets (a gym's "Directly into:" pairs), so the screens group only where there is nothing to preserve: when a section becomes timed or changes format and none of its exercises is grouped (one superset, cap 8); an added or moved exercise joins only under D21's edge. Not triggered by plain edits or by removing the timer, never by `settle`, and the lifter can link and unlink freely. A section of one gets no group; more than 8 shows a muted line. **Rationale:** Carol's rule ("these sets should be supersets, if they aren't already") is about the *absence* of grouping; overriding grouping the gym text or the lifter already made would destroy information. **Rejected:** a server rule (09.5 D7); forcing one superset (Carol overruled it); regrouping on every render; locking the link toggle.
- ✅ **D6 (mine) — The Set Timer's rounds do not resize the exercises' planned sets.** "5 rounds" and "5 sets each" are the same number only by coincidence of how a gym writes it; the routine's planned sets (09.1) stay the lifter's. **Rejected:** deriving one from the other (it would rewrite plans the owner typed).
- ✅ **D7 (mine) — A new `round` / `result` op replaces only the last queued op of the same section and key.** §6.6 explains why folding into an earlier op can create a queue prefix the server refuses. Costs a few extra ops. **Rejected:** the `note` op's "last op for the same exercise anywhere in the queue" (safe there because notes have no neighbors).
- ✅ **D8 (mine) — `orderRoundWrites` queues a Save's round changes as clears, downs ascending, ups / new descending, then the result.** **Rationale:** each write is valid against the previous state, proven by the property test; it removes a class of permanent `422`s on replay. **Rejected:** "clear everything then set ascending" (loses values if a set later fails permanently); sending a whole array (09.5 D11 rejected it).
- ✅ **D9 (mine) — The Timer group is disabled while the section has unsent entries.** A timer change is immediate and online-only; a queued entry validated against the old timer could be refused permanently. **Rejected:** letting the change through and surfacing failures later (a lost score is the anti-goal); queueing timer changes (10.6 D3).
- ✅ **D10 (mine) — The reader keeps the recognized lines in the section note.** Nothing is lost if a rule misreads a line; the editor's header line is the confirmation and the lifter can delete the note line. The three example tests keep their note assertions. **Cost:** the plan appears twice (header and note). **Rejected:** removing the line (a mis-parse then deletes the gym's own text).
- ✅ **D11 (mine) — Score kind from the badge first, the `Score:` line second; AMRAP needs a rounds + reps score.** The badge is the app's explicit field; the line is prose. A conflict (`WEIGHT` badge with a time `Score:` line) is untimed. **Rationale:** matches the owner's "only when its score is a time" and her check that the gym marks AMRAPs with ROUNDS/REPS (fixture 26).
- ✅ **D12 (mine) — A time-scored section with no recognized timing line stays untimed.** A TIME badge alone says "scored by time", not how long or how many rounds; a guessed "For Time" would be wrong for a Set Timer whose line the OCR dropped. The editor lets the lifter set it in two taps.
- ✅ **D13 (mine; reworked on O2) — The import plan keeps the text's own supersets and fills in only when the text gives none.** Second Shift ("21-15-9 … Directly into: 21-15-9 …") stays two supersets of two; a timed section whose lines the reader left ungrouped (bulleted lines) becomes one superset. **Rationale:** the reader's runs already encode what the gym wrote; the same fill-in rule as the editor keeps one behavior everywhere (§6.4).
- ✅ **D14 (mine) — Log result is a save-based sheet, not inline fields.** One tap to open, type, one tap to save, local-first close. **Rationale:** the sheet gives all rounds, the total and the errors in one place and matches the app's sheet pattern (10.6). No autofocus (the `Sheet` rule; iOS ignores programmatic focus outside a gesture anyway), so the first empty row is marked `Next`.

- ✅ **D16 (mine) — History rows show scored timed sections only, at most two lines, then `+n more`.** A plan without a result is not a result; a row of several timed sections stays scannable. (Design §7 left this to the builder.)
- ✅ **D17 (mine) — Defaults are empty.** Picking a format shows empty fields rather than a guessed 5 × 5:00. **Rationale:** a prefilled wrong number is saved without being read; empty fields name what is missing.
- ✅ **D18 (mine; reworked on Carol's F2 answer) — A same-format change that does not contain the recorded times is the same confirm + `discardResult` flow as a format change.** One dialog, one body line, one flag (09.5 AC13/D13); no dead end and no "clear those times first". The client decides with `timerFitsRecorded`, so a change that still fits is sent plainly and keeps the data.
- ✅ **D19 (mine) — No Finish-dialog prompt for a timed section with no result** (O3's default). Anti-goal: friction at the end of a workout.
- ✅ **D21 (mine) — The "join" edge: an added or moved exercise joins only when the section's other exercises are exactly one superset, or are one lone exercise.** Simple to say and to predict: one visible bracket grows by one; two brackets or a mix of bracket and loose means the lifter structured the section, so the new exercise stays loose and she links it by hand. A lone other exercise counts as a group of one so the second exercise of a new timed section pairs with the first, the "if they aren't already" case. **Rejected:** joining the last superset (guesses which of two), always joining the nearest (merges what she kept apart), never joining (a new AMRAP section built one exercise at a time would stay ungrouped).
- ✅ **D20 (mine) — Hevy needs no new UI.** The send path builds the body from the DTO in `@sin/core` (09.5 C); the "Changed since sent" state is the server's; the existing `section_no_placeholder` notice already names sections whose text moved to an exercise's notes. AC62 pins that no web module formats a timer line.
